Streaks

"Which deed is most beloved to Allah?" He said: "The prayer at its time."

Bukhārī 527, Muslim 85

"Whoever prays twelve rakʿahs in a day and night, a house is built for him in Paradise."

Muslim 728: 4 before Ẓuhr and 2 after, 2 after Maghrib, 2 after ʿIshāʾ, 2 before Fajr (Tirmidhī 415)

"The best prayer after the obligatory prayer is the prayer at night."

Muslim 1163

After each prayer, look back over the hours since the last one and check the four doors sins come through (p. 341). It takes a minute. "The wise one holds himself to account and works for what comes after death" (Tirmidhī 2459, book p. 61).

The four filters

The glance · p. 341

The glance leads to everything after it:

glance→thought→idea→desire→craving→resolve→action

"Patience in lowering the gaze is easier than patience with the pain of what comes after it."

The thought · p. 346

Keep every thought within four foundations:

  1. bringing a benefit in this world
  2. repelling a harm in this world
  3. bringing a benefit for the ākhirah
  4. repelling a harm of the ākhirah

Everything else is either a whisper of Shayṭān or an empty wish. Put the most important first. A passing thought does no harm; calling it back does (p. 349).

The word · p. 353

Before you speak, ask:

  1. Is there benefit in it for my dīn? If not, stay silent.
  2. Is there something better to say? Then say that, not the lesser.

"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him say good or stay silent" (Bukhārī 6475, Muslim 47).

The step · p. 361

Take no step towards anything without reward. If a step brings no reward, staying seated is better. A permissible errand done for Allah becomes a step of nearness.

What blocks a du'a

Ḥarām food, drink, clothing

The dusty traveller raising his hands, "so how can he be answered?" (Muslim 1015). p. 11

A heedless heart

"Allah does not answer a du'a from a heedless, distracted heart" (Tirmidhī 3479). Like an arrow from a weak bow. p. 11

Haste

Saying "I asked and asked and was not answered", then giving up (Bukhārī 6340, Muslim 2735). p. 19

Asking for sin

Or for cutting ties of kinship (Muslim 2735). p. 19

Sins and oppression

The darkness of sins over the heart weakens the du'a. p. 11

When the hands stay down

Raising the hands is from the etiquette of du'a (p. 21), but there are moments when the Sunnah is to keep them down.

During the Friday khuṭbah

When the imām makes du'a, the listeners say āmīn quietly, without raising their hands. The imām himself only points with his index finger: ʿUmārah ibn Ruʾaybah objected when a governor raised both hands on the minbar and said the Prophet ﷺ did no more than point with his finger.

Muslim 874

Inside the prayer

Du'a in sujūd, between the two sajdahs and before the salām is made with the hands in their prayer position. Qunūt is the exception many scholars allow, based on reports from ʿUmar and others.

Prayer of the Prophet ﷺ; qunūt: al-Bayhaqī

After the obligatory prayer

The Prophet ﷺ made dhikr and du'a after the prayer, but raising the hands for a du'a after every farḍ prayer, or a group du'a with the imām, is not reported from him. Scholars such as Ibn Bāz advised not to make it a habit.

Fatāwā Ibn Bāz

When the hands go up: du'a outside the prayer ("Your Lord is shy to send back empty the hands His servant raises to Him", Abū Dāwūd 1488, Tirmidhī 3556). In the prayer for rain they go up high, even in the khuṭbah: the Prophet ﷺ raised his hands and the people raised theirs with him (Bukhārī 1029, 1031). Scholars differ on some of these details, so follow what your imām or teacher teaches.

Mūsā asked, Hārūn said āmīn

Mūsā made du'a against Firʿawn (Yūnus 10:88). Allah answered:

قَالَ قَدْ أُجِيبَت دَّعْوَتُكُمَا فَاسْتَقِيمَا

"Your du'a (of you two) has been answered, so remain upright" (Yūnus 10:89). Only Mūsā spoke the du'a, yet Allah said your du'a, of you two. The mufassirūn, as Ibn Kathīr reports from Abū al-ʿĀliyah, ʿIkrimah and others, explain: Mūsā supplicated and Hārūn said āmīn. Whoever says āmīn shares in the du'a.

Two lessons: make du'a together with someone, one asks and the other says āmīn. And the verse ends with fastaqīmā: being answered comes with staying upright.

"When a Muslim makes du'a for his brother in his absence, an angel says: āmīn, and for you the same."

Muslim 2732
